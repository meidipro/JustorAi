## Direct answer

To register land safely, do more than sign a deed. Check the seller, the property records, the chain of ownership, tax information, and any mortgage or dispute. Then prepare the correct deed and present it to the proper registration office within the legal time. After registration, keep the registered copy and update the land record where needed.

## At a glance

- **Who this is for:** A buyer or seller wants the safe legal steps for registering land.
- **Main legal basis:** Registration Act, 1908; Transfer of Property Act, 1882; current Registration amendments.
- **Main rule to remember:** Section 23 of the current Registration Act generally requires a document other than a will to be presented within three months from execution, subject to sections 24–26. The place of registration also matters for land documents.

## Step-by-step

1. Check the seller’s identity and authority to sell.

- What if there is a mortgage? Do not assume it is clear until the relevant records and lender position are checked.

2. Do not call back using a number sent by the caller.
3. Open the official website or app of the claimed organisation.
4. Call the official customer-service or office number.
5. Ask whether the request is genuine.
6. If banking information was exposed, change credentials and contact the provider immediately.
7. Save screenshots of SMS, WhatsApp messages, caller ID and payment instructions.
8. If money was taken or a crime is suspected, make the appropriate complaint to police or another competent authority.
9. If the issue concerns a bank, finance company or MFS provider and remains unresolved, follow Bangladesh Bank's CIPC complaint process.

## Evidence to keep

- Caller number.
- Date and time.
- SMS or WhatsApp messages.
- Name and title claimed by the caller.
- Payment account or MFS number.
- Transaction ID.
- Screenshots.
- Email or website links sent by the caller.
- Bank or provider complaint number.

## Simple example

A caller says, “I am from your bank. Your account will be blocked today. Tell me the six-digit OTP.” The safest action is to end the call. Then contact the bank using its official app, website or card number. If you already shared the OTP, immediately tell the bank that the account may be compromised.

## Common warning signs

- “Act now or your account will be frozen.”
- “You have a police case. Pay now.”
- “Give me the OTP to cancel a transaction.”
- “Send ৳500 first to confirm your identity.”
- “Install this screen-sharing app.”
- “Do not tell anyone about this call.”

## What if...?

- **What if the caller knows my NID number?** Knowing personal information does not prove the caller is genuine.
- **What if the number looks official?** Caller ID can be misleading. Verify through the organisation's official contact details.
- **What if the caller says police will arrest me?** Do not pay money or give account access because of a phone threat. Verify the matter independently.
- **What if I already sent money?** Contact the bank/MFS provider immediately, preserve the transaction details and report the suspected fraud.

## When should you speak to a lawyer or specialist?

Get legal help if there is a large financial loss, identity misuse, a threat of arrest based on a real legal notice, repeated harassment, blackmail, or a dispute about liability after a financial provider rejects your complaint.

## FAQs

### Can a real bank ask for my OTP on a phone call?
Treat any request for a transaction or login OTP as highly sensitive. Verify directly with your bank or MFS provider through its official channel before doing anything.

### The caller knew my address. Does that make the call real?
No. Personal information can be obtained from many sources. Verify the caller independently.

### Can I block the number?
Yes, after saving the evidence you may block the number. Blocking does not replace reporting where money, threats or identity misuse are involved.

### Where do I complain about a bank or MFS issue?
Start with the bank/MFS provider. If unresolved, Bangladesh Bank's CIPC currently accepts complaints and lists hotline 16236, email and an online complaint form.

### Should I delete the messages?
No. Preserve the original messages, numbers and transaction details if you may need to report the incident.

## What if...?

- **What if the money has already been cashed out?** Report it immediately anyway. Recovery is not guaranteed, but delay can make investigation harder.
- **What if I sent the money myself because I was tricked?** It can still be a fraud issue. Explain exactly how you were deceived.
- **What if the provider says the transaction used my PIN?** Record the provider's response and ask for the formal complaint process. Liability can depend on facts and terms.
- **What if the scammer is using another person's MFS account?** Preserve the recipient number and transaction ID. Do not contact or threaten the account holder yourself.

Transaction ID; sender and recipient numbers; account statements; SMS; OTP message; call logs; chat history; screenshots; provider ticket number; police complaint reference where available.

## When should you speak to a lawyer or specialist?

Large loss; disputed provider responsibility; identity theft; repeated account takeover; business account loss; court or criminal process; or a provider complaint that remains unresolved after formal escalation.

### Can Justor promise the money will be recovered?
No. Recovery depends on the facts, timing, transaction path and investigation.

### Is an OTP the same as a PIN?
No. Both are sensitive. A PIN is usually a secret code you choose or receive for account access. An OTP is a temporary one-time code used to verify a login or transaction.

## Direct answer

If you entered a password, card number, PIN, OTP or other sensitive information on a suspicious website, treat that information as exposed. Use the real service's official website or app to change your password and secure the account. Contact the bank or payment provider if financial information was entered. Save the fake URL, message, screenshot and sender information. Do not reopen the link just to collect more evidence.

## What is phishing?

Phishing is a trick that tries to make a person reveal information or install harmful software by pretending to be a trusted organisation or person. The fake message may copy a bank, courier, job site, government portal, social platform or payment service.

Bangladesh's Cyber Security Act, 2026 does not need to use the everyday word “phishing” for the conduct to be legally relevant. Depending on what happens, sections on unauthorised access, cyber forgery and cyber fraud may apply.

BGD e-GOV CIRT has also published national warnings about phishing campaigns and cyber threats. Its official site provides an incident-reporting route for cyber incidents.

## Step-by-step after clicking

1. Stop entering information.
2. Close the page.
3. Use a clean browser or device if you suspect malware.
4. Open the real service by typing its official address yourself.
5. Change the exposed password.
6. Turn on two-factor authentication where available.
7. Check account recovery email and phone numbers.
8. Review recent logins and transactions.
9. Contact your bank/MFS provider if financial information was exposed.
10. Save the original message and URL without revisiting the page.
11. Report serious incidents through the appropriate platform, provider, police or cyber incident channel.

## Warning signs

- A spelling error in the domain name.
- A shortened or strange link.
- “Your account will be closed in 10 minutes.”
- A login page opened from an unexpected SMS.
- A job or prize that asks for card or banking credentials.
- A government page hosted on an unrelated domain.
- A request to install an unknown APK or browser extension.

## Simple example

A text message says, “Your parcel is blocked. Pay ৳30 now,” and links to a page that looks like a courier website. The page asks for card details and an OTP. The user should close the page and contact the courier using its real website. If card data was entered, the bank should be contacted immediately.

## What if...?

- **I clicked but entered nothing.** Risk is lower, but still watch for downloads, permission requests and unusual account activity.
- **I entered only my email.** Be alert for follow-up phishing and account-reset attempts.
- **I entered my password.** Change it immediately on the real service and anywhere else you reused it.
- **I downloaded an app.** Remove suspicious software, review permissions and consider technical support if the device behaves strangely.

## When should you speak to a lawyer or specialist?

Financial loss; business email compromise; stolen confidential data; repeated identity theft; malware affecting a company; or a dispute with a service provider about responsibility.

## FAQs

### Is every suspicious link a crime?
No. The legal issue depends on the sender's conduct and intent. Do not label a person a criminal without evidence.

### Can I report a cyber incident to CIRT?
BGD e-GOV CIRT's official site provides a “Report Incident” function and current contact information for cyber incidents.

### Should I send the link to friends to ask whether it is fake?
Do not spread a suspicious link. Send a screenshot instead where possible.

### Does HTTPS mean a website is safe?
No. HTTPS only shows that the connection is encrypted. A scam site can also use HTTPS.

### What is the best evidence?
The original message, sender, URL, screenshot, time, transaction information and any account-security alert.
